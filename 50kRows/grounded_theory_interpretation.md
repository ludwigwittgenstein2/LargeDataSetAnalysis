"# Executive Summary\n\nThis bulk-coded corpus (50,000 narrative rows aggregated to 1,727 non-redundant evaluation events) reproduces, at scale, the taxonomy generated during an earlier inductive pilot. Several patterns recur with sufficient consistency to be treated as stable descriptive signals, though all are associative, not causal, and self-reported model confidence should not be read as calibrated probability.\n\n1. **Faculty-mediated supervision dominates the environmental narrative.** E01 (Faculty teaching/feedback/mentorship, 96.2%) and E02 (Supervision & graduated autonomy, 57.6%) are the most prevalent environment codes, co-occurring with M01 (Iterative feedback/coaching, 90.2%) and M03 (Direct supervision/scaffolding, 86.0%) — a tight environment–mechanism pairing.\n2. **A large minority of narratives contain no explicit environmental or developmental content.** E15 (\"no explicit learning-environment feature,\" 87.6%) and D10 (\"no explicit developmental effect,\" 62.0%) are both highly prevalent, indicating that much narrative text is evaluative/affective rather than descriptive of mechanism — an important taxonomy-adequacy caveat, not evidence that environment/mechanism is absent.\n3. **Developmental emphasis centers on relational/interpersonal competencies.** D04 (Communication & teamwork, 88.4%) and D06 (Feedback receptivity, 77.1%) outrank D01 (Clinical reasoning, 69.3%) and D02 (Procedural competence, 27.7%), suggesting evaluators (and/or the coding model) foreground relational growth over technical skill acquisition.\n4. **Workload, equity, and systems-barrier codes are modestly but consistently associated with *lower* structured scores**, despite narrative tone remaining positive. Effect sizes are small-to-moderate (odds ratios 0.33–0.66; mean score differences −0.3 to −0.8 points) but statistically robust after FDR correction (e.g., E14 Equity/discrimination: OR 0.33, 95% CI 0.22–0.50, q<0.001; E10 Workflow barriers: OR 0.50, q<0.001; E11 Wellbeing/fatigue: OR 0.55, q<0.001).\n5. **Faculty teaching and psychological safety show no significant structured-score association** (E01 OR 1.46, q=0.35; E09 OR 0.88, q=0.48) despite near-universal prevalence — a dissociation between \"most commonly mentioned\" and \"most score-discriminating.\"\n6. **Narrative sentiment and structured score band are largely decoupled.** Across all three structured-score bands (HIGH/MIDDLE/LOW), roughly 90% of narratives are independently classified as \"HIGH\" sentiment (508/547 when structured=HIGH; 491/547 when structured=LOW), indicating a strong narrative leniency/positivity ceiling that does not track quantitative score variation.\n7. **Temporal (calendar-year) trends are weak and mostly non-significant after multiplicity correction**; only E13 (Assessment/evaluation expectations) survives FDR correction (ρ=−0.70, q=0.026), suggesting a possible decline in explicit mention of assessment processes over the sampled calendar years — notably a calendar trend, not a PGY/training-stage trajectory.\n8. **Longitudinal repeated-evaluatee analysis shows no detectable change** in structured scores over time (mean change −0.016, Wilcoxon p=0.68, sign-test p=0.76; n=180), consistent with either true stability, insufficient power, or non-independence of repeated measures.\n9. **Program-level heterogeneity is substantial** in mean score (4.40–7.12), negative-case rate (5.4%–49.0%), mean confidence (51.2–70.6), and gap rate (0–19.6%), indicating program/evaluator clustering is a major candidate confound requiring mixed-effects modeling before any cross-program claims.\n10. **Confidence and adjudication burden are non-trivial**: mean row confidence 62.5 (median 72), 8,412/50,000 rows (16.8%) below threshold, and 1,059/1,727 events (61.3%) flagged for adjudication — far higher than the taxonomy-gap rate (0.37%), implying most uncertainty is about *application* of existing codes rather than *missing* codes.\n\n# Core Category\n\n**Provisional core category: \"Faculty-Mediated Supervised Participation Under Workload Strain.\"**\n\nThis category integrates the environment → mechanism → development chain observed across the dataset: the environmental backbone is faculty presence, feedback, and graduated supervision (E01, E02, E07) operating inside a high-volume, high-acuity clinical workload context (E03, E11, E12); the mechanism by which this environment is said to act is iterative feedback, direct supervision/scaffolding, and reflective self-assessment (M01, M03, M06); and the developmental outcomes most strongly implicated are relational/interpersonal (D04, D06) more than technical (D01, D02). The \"strain\" qualifier is retained because the same workload/systems/equity codes that frequently co-occur with the supervisory core are the only codes showing a statistically detectable (if modest) negative association with structured performance scores — even though narrative sentiment remains uniformly positive. This suggests the environment functions simultaneously as a developmental scaffold and a strain-inducing moderator, and that these two faces are visible only when triangulating structured and narrative signals.\n\nThis core category is **provisional and pending human adjudication** — it was derived from descriptive co-occurrence and association patterns in model-generated codes, not from direct re-reading of source narratives, and roughly 61% of events are flagged for human review before this integration can be trusted as a theoretical claim.\n\n# Axial Coding Model\n\n**Causal/conditioning context**\n- High clinical workload, volume & complexity (E03, 68.3%); patient-care acuity/continuity (E12, 55.7%); teaching infrastructure/didactics (E07, 81.6%); program-level variation in culture/resources (program summary heterogeneity).\n\n**Central phenomenon**\n- Faculty-mediated supervised clinical participation — graduated autonomy delivered through direct supervision and iterative feedback, embedded in day-to-day clinical workflow (E01+E02 co-occurring with M01+M03 in the large majority of events).\n\n**Actions/interactions/mechanisms**\n- Iterative feedback, coaching & modeling (M01, 90.2%); direct supervision & scaffolding (M03, 86.0%); reflection/self-assessment/goal-setting (M06, 77.4%); structured teaching/didactics/simulation (M07, 66.2%); autonomy/ownership/responsibility (M04, 61.1%); team/interprofessional interaction (M05, 40.5%).\n\n**Consequences/developmental outcomes**\n- Communication & teamwork development (D04, 88.4%); feedback receptivity & self-directed learning (D06, 77.1%); clinical reasoning (D01, 69.3%); autonomy/confidence (D03, 66.9%); professional identity/leadership (D05, 54.6%); wellbeing/resilience (D09, 46.8%) — the last suggesting the strain context has its own, separately coded, developmental consequence (coping), distinct from skill acquisition.\n\n**Contextual moderators**\n- Psychological safety & relational climate (E09, 74.7%) and wellbeing/fatigue (E11, 64.2%) moderate how workload and supervision are experienced; equity/discrimination climate (E14, 16.2%) and workflow/systems barriers (E10, 26.0%), though less prevalent, carry the largest negative structured-score associations; program/evaluator identity is a strong structural moderator (score range 4.4–7.1 across programs); evaluation-type (faculty-of-resident vs. patient/staff vs. self) moderates feedback specificity/actionability.\n\n# Constant Comparison and Negative Cases\n\n**High vs. low structured score contrast.** Codes describing supervision, workload, patient responsibility, assessment burden, and especially equity/discrimination climate are all *more* prevalent among low-scoring events than high-scoring events (e.g., E14 present in 22.2% of low-score events vs. 8.5% of high-score events, OR 0.33). This is a genuine negative case relative to the dominant \"supervision is uniformly developmental\" narrative: when equity/discrimination or systems-barrier themes surface, they travel with lower, not higher, structured ratings. Faculty teaching (E01) and psychological safety (E09), despite being near-ubiquitous, show *no* significant score discrimination — i.e., their presence is necessary-sounding but not discriminating, a pattern consistent with ceiling effects or near-universal positive framing of faculty mentorship regardless of quality.\n\n**Perspective differences.** Feedback specificity/actionability varies by evaluator type: faculty-of-resident and resident-of-faculty narratives are the most specific/actionable (specificity ~3.1–3.3), while faculty-of-program/hospital evaluations are least specific (2.78) — suggesting institution-level feedback is more generic than individual-level feedback.\n\n**Temporal differences.** Only one code (E13, assessment/evaluation expectations) shows an FDR-robust calendar-year trend (declining mention, ρ=−0.70). All other apparent trends (E01, E09, E11, E02, E05, E08) have moderate rho but do not survive multiplicity correction — these should be treated as exploratory, not confirmed, and in no case should they be read as PGY/training-stage trajectories, since the data are calendar-year, not longitudinally tracked by training level.\n\n**Taxonomy gaps.** The gap rate is low (0.37% of rows), and most flagged \"gap concepts\" are near-duplicates of the existing \"no explicit learning-environment feature\" code rather than genuinely novel constructs. A small number of singleton gap concepts (e.g., \"disorganized rotation with conflicting supervision,\" \"degrading attitude, poor psychological safety,\" \"gender-inclusive communication and listening clarity\") are substantively interesting negative/adverse cases that do not fit cleanly into the current 15-code environment taxonomy and merit qualitative follow-up, even though they are numerically rare.\n\n**Low-confidence cases.** 16.8% of rows fall below the confidence threshold, and 61.3% of events require adjudication — far exceeding the taxonomy-gap rate. This indicates uncertainty is concentrated in *code application* (ambiguous narratives, borderline category boundaries) rather than in *missing categories*, an important distinction for interpreting the \"saturation\" question below.\n\n**Adverse/negative cases.** Rare but clinically important sensitizing constructs — discrimination_bias (4.2%), error_disclosure (3.4%), serious_patient_outcome (5.7%), handoff_systems_failure (9.4%) — are infrequent but should not be dismissed as noise; their low base rate combined with the near-ubiquitous \"positive\" performance signal (91.7%) raises the concern that adverse events are under-captured or under-weighted in the dominant narrative, a pattern consistent with evaluator leniency bias rather than genuine absence of risk.\n\n**Contradiction to the dominant pattern.** The narrative-vs-structured agreement table is the clearest contradiction to a simple \"environment drives development, narrative reflects score\" story: narrative sentiment is classified HIGH in ~90% of events *regardless* of whether the structured score band is HIGH, MIDDLE, or LOW. This means narrative tone is not a reliable proxy for structured performance differentiation in this corpus.\n\n# Theoretical Saturation / Taxonomy"
